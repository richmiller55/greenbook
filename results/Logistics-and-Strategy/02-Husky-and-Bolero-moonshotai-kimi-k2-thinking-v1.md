## 5. Strategic Discussion Questions (Answer Key)

1. **British vs. American Viewpoints at Washington Conference (Trident, May 1943):** The British delegation advocated for Mediterranean operations (HUSKY) as a direct path to "close with the enemy," viewing Italy as the "soft underbelly" and prioritizing operational momentum. The American Joint Chiefs, particularly Admiral King and General Marshall, saw BOLERO as the non-negotiable main effort for a direct cross-channel invasion (OVERLORD), viewing Mediterranean operations as a diversion that bled resources from the decisive theater. The compromise was the "56 LST withdrawal"—a temporary sacrifice from ETO to enable HUSKY, with explicit JCS stipulations that these assets would return to UK by October 1943.

2. **Role of "Anvil" (Later "Dragoon") Debate:** The Anvil concept—an Allied landing in southern France—was the *bridge* between HUSKY and BOLERO. In early 1943, Anvil served as the strategic justification for Mediterranean operations: it promised a path from Italy back to the decisive theater. However, the landing craft required for Anvil (estimated 300+ LSTs) created a circular dependency: HUSKY diverted craft from BOLERO, which delayed OVERLORD, which in turn postponed Anvil. This debate crystallized the core tension: Mediterranean operations consumed the very lift capacity needed to execute follow-on strategic options, turning logistical constraints into strategic paralysis. The JCS insisted Anvil must not delay OVERLORD, which ultimately meant HUSKY's craft had to be returned to UK by late 1943—a timeline that proved politically and logistically unworkable.
