### Strategic Discussion Questions

1. **British vs. American viewpoints at the Washington Conference (Trident, May 1943)**  
   The British chief aim was to **maintain momentum in the Mediterranean**—they argued that Operation HUSKY would knock Italy out of the war, force Germany to divert forces south, and open the Mediterranean to Allied shipping, thereby freeing tonnage. They viewed the Mediterranean as a low-risk, high-reward area that would eventually benefit the cross-Channel invasion. The Americans, led by General Marshall and Admiral King, insisted that **BOLERO (the build-up) and the cross-Channel invasion (OVERLORD) must remain the primary strategic objective**. They feared that operations in the Mediterranean were a "suction pump" that would never end and would delay the decisive blow against Germany. At the Trident Conference, the Americans agreed to proceed with HUSKY but extracted a **firm commitment to OVERLORD as a matter of priority**, with a target date of May 1944—partly to reassure the USSR and to prevent endless Mediterranean adventures paddling resources away from the main effort.

2. **Role of the "Anvil" (later "Dragoon") debate in landing craft allocation disputes**  
   The Anvil plan—an invasion of Southern France designed to support OVERLORD by drawing German forces away from Normandy—**intensified the landing craft shortage**. Operation HUSKY (July 1943) had already stripped LSTs from the BOLERO pool. After HUSKY, planners argued over whether those LSTs should return to the UK or be kept in the Mediterranean for a follow-up invasion of Italy (AVALANCHE) and eventually for ANVIL. The Americans pushed hard for ANVIL, seeing it as necessary to OVERLORD’s success; the British, preferring an Italian campaign and seeing ANVIL as a waste of resources, resisted. This debate **directly drove the allocation of LSTs** between the Mediterranean and the UK, creating a tug-of-war that delayed BOLERO and forced the revision of assault schedules for OVERLORD. The constant renegotiation illustrated the acute scarcity of amphibious shipping and shaped the final compromise: ANVIL was postponed until August 1944 (re‑named DRAGOON), but the decision to conduct it further delayed the return of LSTs to the UK, which had cascading effects on the Overlord build-up.
